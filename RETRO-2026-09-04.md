## The finding that matters: tests stayed green through every real defect

Thirteen `npm run verify` scripts and fifteen Playwright e2e tests were passing
before this session and after it. Not one of the defects below was caught by
them. Every one was found by using the product, or by pointing something at it
that had not been pointed at it before.

### Found by giving the tools to a real model

1. **`TOOL_MANIFEST` advertised 18 tools with no `inputSchema` at all.**
   `/api/v1/tools` was serving an uncallable API to any REST client, and the
   agent runner handed models an empty parameter schema. Two of five frontier
   models called `add_to_order({})` and failed the shopping task. They were
   correct. We had told them the tool took no arguments.
2. **`get_product` took `id`, `add_to_order` took `product_id`.** A model called
   `get_product({id})`, got a product back, carried `id` forward, and looped six
   times on `Product not found:` with an empty value. Our own tool surface
   trapped an agent, on a product whose thesis is making stores legible to
   agents.
3. **`search_catalog("espresso beans")` returned zero** from a store selling
   "House Espresso Blend", because the query matched as one substring. Gemini
   2.5 Pro concluded the store had nothing and told the shopper so. GPT-5.4
   recovered only by retrying with a shorter query.

### Found by reading our own output as a stranger would

4. **The field companion quoted 31% and 22%** while the scored receipt charged
   the cited 24% and 15%. Two numbers for the same thing, in a product whose
   pitch is that every number names its source.
5. **A crawl-only audit returned `summary.fullScore: 78`** while four checkout
   lines sat in `unmeasuredLineIds` and the product said crawls never yield a
   /100.
6. **The census wall painted 70 stores one grey.** Two different facts were
   hiding there: 48 that answered and sent nothing, and 22 that refused the
   request outright. Painting "we could not look" as "you have no data" is the
   exact error this product exists to catch in other people's dashboards.

### Found by looking at pixels instead of code

7. **`.main-layout` reserved 21rem for a sidebar that exists on one tab**, so
   two of three tabs rendered a permanently empty third of the page.
8. **Five font weights and about thirty sizes**, twenty of them inside a 0.3rem
   band. `.integrations h2` out-specified the front door headline, so the
   wordmark rendered larger than the sentence the page exists to say.
9. **All nine screenshots were the old mint skin**, stale against production.

### Found by checking the artifact rather than the build log

10. **The film mixed two narration paragraphs on top of each other for 6.9
    seconds.** Cues were a hardcoded dict. `lay_voice.py` printed that exact
    warning and exited 0, so nobody read it.
11. **The outro card was 2.0s under a 9.6s closing paragraph**, so the last line
    played on a dead frame.
12. **Beats measured elapsed time with `performance.now()`**, which restarts on
    navigation, so every beat over-held: 148s of picture over 83s of voice. The
    guard only failed when the picture was too short.
13. **The fix CSV would have matched nothing.** Shopify imports match by
    `Handle`. We stored the SKU as the product id and never captured the handle.
    The export would have looked like it worked.

**The pattern.** A green suite proves the code does what the test says. It says
nothing about whether the thing is usable, legible, or honest. Thirteen of these
were invisible to it.

## The measurement that closed a strategic question

22 of 148 storefronts refuse our crawler. The question was whether to route
around it. Answered with two experiments rather than an opinion:

- Six declared identities (no UA, ReadyCounterBot, GPTBot, ClaudeBot,
  PerplexityBot, full Chrome 152): **0 of 14**, identical status distribution.
  The user agent is not the signal.
- Real Chrome, real TLS, real cookies, fetching from inside the page's own
  origin: **0 of 22**.

So a VPS makes it worse, because datacenter ASN is the block signal, and
residential proxies would work while ending the product the first time anyone
noticed. The legitimate route is Web Bot Auth: an Ed25519 key, a JWKS at
`/.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory`, `Signature-Agent` headers, and
Cloudflare's signed agent list. A product arguing that stores should be able to
identify honest agents should be one. Full writeup:
`research/AGENT-ACCESS-AND-THE-FIX-PATH.md`.

## What went right, and is worth repeating

**Ideation ran long on purpose and paid.** The best thing in the submission, the
fix export, did not come from the backlog. It came from a conversation at the
end about whether the product was actually useful to a shop owner, which
produced the honest answer of "not yet, it stops at advice", which produced the
CSV. Cost: about ninety minutes of talking. Return: the difference between a
scoreboard and a tool.

**Numbers were probed, not recalled.** Model IDs came from listing what the key
could see. Shopify's `barcode` field came from the docs. Cloudflare's programme
came from the source. The one time a finding rested on stored data instead of a
fresh probe, "0 of 22 blocking stores expose UCP", it turned out to be an
artifact of our own crawler being blocked.

**Defects were fixed at the cause.** The overlapping audio became derived cues
plus a fatal check. The missing schemas became a generated map plus a build
guard. Three `verify` scripts were added, each one failing the build if a defect
of that class returns.

## What to fix next time

**The write rules were in memory and still broken.** The submission went out
full of em dashes despite a hard ban recorded on 2026-07-22. Recall is not a
gate. The rule is now "before writing, check the write rules", and the checklist
is in the vault.

**The pack drifted from the source.** `05-text/` in Downloads was edited in
place and synced once, so the YouTube description Oscar nearly pasted still had
the pre-rename URLs. Now copied from the repo every time.

**Six of ten gallery images were seven hours stale** at the point they were
about to be uploaded, showing a header that no longer existed. Any artifact
built from a live surface needs a rebuild trigger tied to that surface changing.

**Model latency was treated as a bug for too long.** Three film rebuilds were
spent trying to make the cold open reach the CAPTCHA before accepting that the
narration should describe only what the picture reliably shows.
